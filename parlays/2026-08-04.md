### Tier 1 — best standalone +EV play

> ### ⭐ **Grant Holmes UNDER 4.5 strikeouts — −119 (best of book, BetMGM/DK tier)**
> **TrueP 62.1% · no-vig 52.1% · edge +10.0pp** (vig-inclusive break-even 54.3% → **+7.8pp**)
> `[adj: custom+10]` · EV **+14.3%** · ¼-Kelly **2.00u (capped)** · bucket **STANDALONE**

**Why this and not a K-Over.** Holmes' K distribution is unusually tight and low: **≤4 K in 15 of 21
starts (71.4%), and 8 of his last 10 (80%)**. He is a 7.68 K/9 arm averaging **4.9 IP per start** — the
combination that makes a 4.5 line an Under, not a coin flip. A Poisson on the season rate (λ=4.18) gives
59.4%; the empirical rate gives 71.4%; his counts are under-dispersed (**exactly 4 K in 9 of 21 starts**),
which is why the empirical sits above the model. I registered **62.1%**, below both the empirical read and
my first pass, deliberately.

**This is the thesis the ledger rewards.** `fades.md` C1's 8/3 refinement says to prefer the
**start-length** thesis over the **contact-lineup** thesis when choosing a K-Under — this leg is pure
start-length (4.9 IP/start, 7.68 K/9) and carries no contact-lineup premise at all: MIA sits at a neutral
**21% K vs RHP**. `type:K-Under` is the warmest dimension in the pulse window (**5-2, 71% vs 53% claimed**).

**Why it clears the 8/1 variance-diversify rule.** That rule says a K-prop takes Tier 1 only when its edge
*clearly exceeds* every alternative, and never at a 7.5 standard line. This is a **4.5** line, and +10.0pp
against the best non-K alternative (+3.8pp) is not a close call.

**The honest risk:** one fat right tail. Holmes has a 10-K start in the sample (1 of 21). A single dominant
outing busts this leg outright, and no amount of start-length reasoning protects against it.
